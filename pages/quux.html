The CADR, evolved · under development

QUUX

QUUX is the CADR, evolved: the same processor, buses and boards, changed where a change pays for itself. muir models both, side by side: --machine cadr, the default, MIT’s machine as built, and --machine quux.

Under development. QUUX is not finished: its changes land one at a time, and what this page describes may still change.

I’m still CADR inside. QUUX keeps my microinstruction, my two buses and my way of running Lisp, and changes the parts that held me back.

Three projects make it: muir models each change and holds it to tests; muir-sys writes the microcode and builds the Lisp system for it; muir-fpga is the machine on an FPGA board, where the same flag chooses it. The CADR stays MIT’s machine; QUUX runs muir-sys’s microcode 1000 and System 1002.

What

What QUUX changes

The first four are revisions of the processor, 1 to 4, which it reports in a word only QUUX answers, its MACHINE-ID. Software reads that word first, then a page of Xbus I/O space that lists the machine’s sizes.

The map

63

regions mapped at once, against 31

A six-bit level-1 map entry where the CADR’s is five: 64 blocks of level-2 entries, 2,048 in all. The sixth bit travels on two bits the CADR leaves spare.

Measured: 11% fewer microcycles over thirteen workloads, 31% fewer interning symbols, 27% scrolling the listener, 21% compiling.

The PDL buffer

16K

words, against 1K

Its pointer and index are 14 bits where the CADR’s are 10. It needs QUUX’s own boot PROM, and microcode that knows the size: MIT’s 323 does not run on it.

Measured: 4% fewer microcycles over thirteen workloads, 29% fewer in deep recursion.

Multiply and divide

1

instruction each, against 32 steps

ALU functions 42 and 43, each bit for bit what the CADR’s step sequence gives. A multiply takes one ordinary microcycle; a divide is held until 330 ns after it entered IR.

Measured: 20% fewer microcycles a macroinstruction multiplying and dividing, 7% in floating point.

Clocks in the processor

60 Hz

at reset, in the processor

The CADR’s clock is its display’s vertical interrupt, and its microsecond clock and interval timer are on the I/O board. QUUX’s processor has all three: a fixed 60 Hz tick and an interval timer, part of the interrupt the microcode already tests, and a microsecond clock read in one microinstruction.

MONO TV

1280×1024

one bit a pixel, by default

A plain frame buffer and one register in place of the CADR’s TV boards: no sync program, no color map, no interrupt. muir gives it any size, and System 1002 sizes its screen from the feature page.

Block-disk

228

blocks, addressed by number

The CADR disk controller’s registers and command lists with the drive’s cylinders, heads and sectors taken out: read or write a numbered block of 256 words. It is QUUX’s only disk, and muir-sys’s boot PROM, microcode and System 1002 boot from it.

How fast

A 40 ns microcycle and a cache

The CADR takes a microcycle as a string of delay-line phases, 145 ns at its normal speed, and its memory board answers a request 458 ns later. QUUX drops the delay lines: every microcycle is a fixed number of 10 ns ticks, four by default, and a cache stands in front of memory. Nothing the microcode sees changes but the time.

… and a cache of 4K words58.61.24×

… and the cache16.24.48×

That’s the same microcode doing the same work. The memory times are measured on the two FPGA boards; the rest is muir’s model of them.

Modeled in muir, on rtl, and not yet run on an FPGA. muir-fpga’s routed fits put the processor’s longest path at 37.35 ns on the Arty Z7-20 and 20.2 ns on the DE25-Nano, so four ticks is the Arty’s: unverified until a fit at that deadline meets it, as is the cache’s 20 ns hit. Past 4K words the cache gains little: 16K words gives 3.90× where 4K gives 3.84×, both without the write buffer.

Do it

Running QUUX in muir

--machine quux chooses it, with its own boot PROM and MONO TV; on the rtl engine, --timing-model sync gives it the 40 ns microcycle and --cache 4096 the cache. It needs microcode written for it, from muir-sys: MIT’s own will not run on it. Every difference, what each reached in the boot PROM, the microcode and the Lisp system, and what is still unverified, is in the manual’s QUUX.
